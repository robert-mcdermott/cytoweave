Flow cytometry analysis you can check

CytoWeave is an analysis workbench for conventional, spectral and mass cytometry. It runs on your own computer as one self-contained program, installed with a single command: no licence server, no account, no Python, R or plugins. Files are read and analyzed in your browser and never leave your machine.

Why CytoWeave

Private by design

FCS files are parsed and analyzed in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, and CytoWeave makes no network requests of its own.

Self-contained

One program for macOS, Linux and Windows, installed with one command. No licence server, account, Python, R or plugins, and no administrator rights.

Checked at every step

Each step checks itself: the compensation against its controls, gates across samples, maps for faithfulness. The whole pipeline is validated against flowCore, FlowKit, PeacoQC, FlowSOM, CytoNorm and FlowJo.

Ready to publish

Live publication figures, a methods paragraph with references written from what you did, and a MIFlowCyt checklist.

What it does

The analysis loop of a cytometry lab, in one place

Compensate or unmix, clean the acquisition, gate the cohort consistently, explore it with clustering and maps, compare groups, and leave with figures and a methods paragraph.

Gating

Gate the whole cohort, adjust one sample

Draw rectangle, polygon, ellipse, freehand, quadrant, range and split gates, or let the magic wand take the density basin under the cursor. Gates are shared by every sample; adjusting one for a single sample makes a visible override, not a copy.

  • The gating path of any population as a row of plots
  • Logicle, arcsinh, log and FlowJo's biexponential, reproduced exactly
  • Backgating, overlays and Boolean populations
Gating
Compensation

A spillover matrix checked against its controls

Compute spillover from single-stain controls by median difference or robust regression, or bring the file's matrix. Then check it: each control is compensated and what its positives still leave in other detectors is measured. A residual means the value is off by about that much, and CytoWeave suggests the correction.

  • Autofluorescent positives, such as dead cells, recognized as such
  • The spillover spreading matrix and N×N pair plots
  • Corrections in one undoable step
Compensation
Spectral

Unmixing, and the evidence to choose a model

Reference spectra are gated from the single-stain controls automatically, and several autofluorescence signatures are extracted from the unstained control. Unmix by ordinary, weighted or non-negative least squares, with per-event autofluorescence.

  • Complexity index, similarity and spreading matrices
  • Seven unmixing models compared on your own sample
  • A residual channel that shows what the references miss
Spectral unmixing
Acquisition QC

Find clogs and bubbles, leave clean data alone

PeacoQC, a flow-rate check, margin events and per-channel drift, with a 0–100 score per sample and plain-language findings. CytoWeave's refined PeacoQC stops the published algorithm removing events from clean or slowly drifting files; the classic port is one click away.

  • A "QC pass" channel to gate on; your data are never changed
  • A cohort overview of every sample
  • The classic algorithm removes the same events as PeacoQC in R
Acquisition QC
Instrument

Know your cytometer: Q, B and every run since

Measure every detector's efficiency Q and optical background B from multi-level beads or an LED pulser, with the weighted quadratic fit of Parks et al. that flowQB uses, and get flowQB's answers. Keep the runs in the instrument's record and follow them on Levey–Jennings charts: a failing PMT, a dirty flow cell or a weak laser shows on the day it happens.

  • Westgard rules against a baseline of runs
  • A spectral library that catches a degraded tandem
  • Library spectra for dyes you have no control for
Q, B and Levey–Jennings
Clustering and maps

Maps that say how far to trust them

FlowSOM, Leiden, Louvain or k-means clustering, and UMAP, t-SNE or PCA across samples. Clusters are named from their marker enrichment and become gateable populations. Every map reports its trustworthiness, continuity, neighbour preservation, sample mixing and seed stability.

  • Shading of unreliable regions on the map
  • Later samples placed on a finished UMAP
  • Cluster abundance straight into group comparisons
Clustering and maps
Review

Every sample's result for a gate, outliers first

Review a gate across the cohort: samples are ranked by how unusual their frequency is, by whether the boundary cuts through dense events, and by low counts. Boundary robustness shows how much a frequency depends on exactly where the gate was drawn.

  • Robust z-scores against the cohort
  • Click a sample to adjust the gate for it alone
  • Overrides stay visible in the population tree
Review across samples
Autogating

Gates that follow drifting samples, and say when they can't

Adapt a gate to every sample: CytoWeave learns from the samples you drew, adjusted or confirmed it on, carries it across by the density peaks of the parent population, and gives each sample a confidence. Confident adjustments are ticked for you to apply; uncertain samples come first, with the reason. A gate that sits in a valley stays, because populations also move for biological reasons.

  • One gate per donor for stimulation assays
  • Checked against an expert's own gates in a real cytokine study
  • Membership probabilities for every event, as CLR
Adapting a gate to each sample
Statistics

The right test for the design

Statistics tables of every population across samples, and group comparisons that pick the test from the design: Welch's t-test, paired t-test, Welch's ANOVA or repeated-measures ANOVA, each with its nonparametric counterpart, effect sizes and bootstrap confidence intervals.

  • Paired designs detected from a subject field
  • Screens of every population or cluster with a volcano plot
  • Benjamini–Hochberg, Benjamini–Yekutieli, Holm or Bonferroni
Tables and comparisons
AI agents

Let an agent gate while you watch, then decide

CytoWeave is an MCP server, so Claude Code and other agents can open data, gate, compute statistics, review gates, compare groups and write the methods, in the window you are watching. Their changes arrive as proposals: new gates appear marked as proposed with real counts, and renames, deletions and compensation matrices wait for you.

  • Accept or reject the whole proposal from one strip
  • The change log records which agent proposed what
  • Every change can be undone
AI agents (MCP)
Moving from FlowJo

Bring your FlowJo workspaces, and see that they match

FlowJo 10 workspaces import with their samples, gates, compensation and scales. The import lists every population as exact, approximated or unsupported, and the migration report compares every population count with FlowJo's, with the likely cause of any difference.

  • FlowJo's biexponential reproduced from its own algorithm
  • Workspaces exported back to FlowJo, one gating tree per sample
  • De-identified FCS files for sharing, with the events untouched
  • Gating-ML 2.0 in and out: all 190 compliance gates exact
  • Archival Cytometry Standard containers and CLR
FlowJo and Gating-ML
Reproducible

Figures, methods and checkpoints

Build the gating strategy of a population in one click, or lay out plots across samples; they stay live until you export SVG, PNG or a 300 dpi PDF. The Report view writes a methods paragraph with numbered references from what the workspace actually did, and checkpoints compare two versions of an analysis in plain language.

  • Exported figures carry the analysis that made them; open one to see what changed
  • Every algorithm with its parameters and seed
  • BibTeX for the references, and a MIFlowCyt checklist
  • "The Live gate moved by 3% of the axis", with its effect on every frequency

And more

Built for the work a core and a lab actually do

Index sorting

The plate of sorted cells, each well colored by its cell's population; click a well to find its cell on every plot.

Cell cycle and proliferation

Dean–Jett–Fox and Watson fits of DNA content; generations of dye dilution with division and proliferation indices.

Batches and barcodes

CytoNorm normalization with a confounding check, bead normalization for mass cytometry, and debarcoding into one sample per well.

Boolean populations

The events in all of, any of or none of chosen populations, with a live count while you choose.

Ten million events

Files are read in parts and never held whole, populations are bitsets, and background analyses share the events instead of copying them.

Ten example experiments

Simulated with the truth of every event: a planted compensation error, a FlowJo workspace, a spectral panel, a barcoded plate, a month of bead QC and more.
